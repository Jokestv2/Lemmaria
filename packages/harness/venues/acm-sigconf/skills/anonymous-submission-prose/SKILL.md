# Prose for a double-blind ACM submission

This venue reviews double-blind under a page limit. Both facts change how the prose is
written, not just how it is formatted. The numbers and the class options are in this
venue's `requirements.md`; what follows is what they mean for the sentences.

## Anonymized phrasing

1. **Cite your own prior work in the third person.** "As shown in prior work [12]",
   never "as we showed in [12]" or "in our earlier paper". The citation stays; the
   ownership goes.
2. **Do not describe the work as extending "our system".** Name the system, or name
   the method, and let the citation carry the lineage.
3. **Keep institutions, funders and named collaborators out of the running text**, not
   only out of the acknowledgements. A dataset described as "collected at our
   university's hospital" de-anonymizes as effectively as a byline.
4. **Do not point at a repository, a demo URL or a preprint that carries your names**
   during review. If the artifact matters to the claim, describe it and say the link
   will be added on acceptance.
5. **Watch the incidental identifiers**: an internal cluster's name, a proprietary
   system nobody else runs, a unique dataset only your group has.

## Writing to a page limit

6. **Cut examples before you cut claims.** A second illustration of a point already
   made is the cheapest page on the paper; a stated limitation is the most expensive
   thing to lose.
7. **The contribution statement never gets shortened.** If the paper runs long, the
   contribution list is the last paragraph to touch and the first a reviewer reads.
8. **Move detail to the appendix rather than deleting it**, and say in the main text
   where it went, so the claim stays checkable.
9. **Long related-work paragraphs compress by grouping**, not by dropping citations —
   one sentence per group of works beats one clause per work.
